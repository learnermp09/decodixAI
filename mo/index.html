Quick Recall Sheet

Core definitions

Microbiology: study of microorganisms.
Food microbiology: role of microorganisms in food spoilage, production, preservation and food-borne disease.
Taxonomy: biological classification; the unit describes classification, nomenclature and identification.
Probiotic: live microorganisms associated with health benefits.

Bacterial shapes

Bacilli = rod-shaped • Cocci = spherical • Vibrio = comma-shaped • Spirillum = spiral-shaped.

Food roles

Desirable: fermentation, food bioprocessing, additives, enzymes, biopreservation, probiotics. Undesirable: food spoilage and food-borne disease.

High-yield organism associations

A. flavus → aflatoxin • A. oryzae → sake starch hydrolysis • A. niger → citric acid/enzymes • R. stolonifer → black bread mold • S. cerevisiae → baking/alcoholic fermentation • Rhodotorula → pigment/discoloration • Pseudomonads → major contaminant of freshly dressed poultry skin.

Growth/environment groups

Thermophilic → 50°C and above • Psychrotrophic → refrigerated temperature (<5°C) • Thermoduric → survive pasteurization temperature • Halotolerant → survive high salt (>10%) • Aciduric → survive pH below 4 • Aerobe → oxygen required • Anaerobe → cannot grow with oxygen • Facultative anaerobe → both.

Food-specific recall

Meat: Pseudomonads, Staphylococci, Micrococci, Enterococci, Coliforms. Poultry: Pseudomonads major contaminant. Egg: fresh interior usually free of microorganisms. Fruits: acidic, restrict bacterial growth but not fungal growth. Milk: excellent growth medium; pasteurization leaves thermoduric organisms.

History anchors

1665 Redi • 1683 Leeuwenhoek • 1765 Spallanzani • 1795 Appert/canning • 1837 Schwann • 1857 Pasteur/milk souring • 1860 Pasteur/heat treatment and pasteurization • early 1920s botulism outbreaks and 12D process.
